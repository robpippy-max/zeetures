# Getting started

## Welcome

Zeetures runs the world of Creatures 2 from your own copy of the game, with creatures that have a mind of their own. Nothing here is scripted play: norns, grendels, and ettins feel needs, learn what eases them, remember places and one another, talk, fall out, breed, catch colds, and grow old. Your part is to look after them, teach them, and watch.

### Your first hour

- **Hatch someone.** Open the [[Hatchery]], choose an egg, and press Put in the incubator. It hatches at once.
- **Find it.** Click the creature to select it, and press Follow on the toolbar so that the view keeps it in sight. See [[Looking round the world]].
- **Feed it.** Babies learn what food is by trying it and by watching others. Put food beside it with the right mouse button. See [[Food and hunger]].
- **Teach it.** Press the button of a teaching machine while it is near, or type a word and press Return. See [[Talking to creatures]].
- **Reward it.** Click its head to tickle it, or lower down to slap it. See [[The hand]].
- **Read it.** The [[Mind Kit]] shows what it feels and why it does what it does. The [[Health Kit]] shows how its body is.
- **Let it meet others.** Hatch a few more, of both sexes. See [[Relationships]] and [[Breeding and eggs]].

### Using this Help

- Choose a topic on the left, or type in the search box: every topic with all of your words in it is listed, and the words are marked in the text.
- Underlined names in the text are links to other topics. Back returns to the topic before.
- F1 opens this window. With a kit open and in front, F1 opens that kit's own topic.

## Keys and mouse

### The mouse

- **Left click:** use a thing (press a button, start a machine, call a lift). On a creature: select it, and tickle or slap it.
- **Right click:** pick a thing or a creature up; right-click again to put it down.
- **Wheel, or two fingers on a touchpad:** move the view in any direction.
- **Middle button, held and dragged:** pull the world about.
- **Ctrl and wheel, or a two-finger pinch:** zoom about the pointer.
- **Scroll bars:** move the view (View > Scroll Bars shows or hides them).

### The keyboard

- **Any letters, then Return:** the hand speaks. Backspace corrects.
- **Ctrl+S:** say the last thing you typed again.
- **Arrow keys:** move the view.
- **Ctrl+0:** zoom back to actual size.
- **F1:** this Help.
- **F12:** full screen, and back.

Your letters go to the world unless a box in a kit or a window has the keyboard. If nothing appears when you type, click on the world first.

## Looking round the world

Albia is much larger than the window, and it has no ends: go far enough east and you come back in the west.

- **Moving the view:** the wheel, the arrow keys, the middle button, or the scroll bars.
- **Zooming:** Ctrl and wheel, a pinch, the Zoom list on the Favourites toolbar, or the View menu; from 50% to 300%.
- **Next:** the toolbar's Next button selects the next creature and goes to it. The Creatures menu lists every living creature by name.
- **Follow:** Follow on the toolbar (Camera > Track Norn) keeps the selected creature in view. Choose another creature, however you do it, and the view follows that one instead. Moving the view yourself stops it.
- **Halo:** shows a halo over the selected creature, so that you can find it in a crowd.
- **Places:** press + beside Places on the Favourites toolbar to name the spot you are looking at (six at most), and choose it from the list to go back. Camera > Remove Favourite Place takes one off.
- **Smooth Scrolling** (Camera menu): the view glides to places and creatures instead of jumping.
- **Smooth Movement** (Camera menu): creatures move smoothly from pose to pose, instead of jumping five times a second. Every breed has it. Turn it off for the original look.
- **ToolTips for Objects** (View menu): names whatever the hand rests on.

## Worlds and saving

### Worlds

Each world is its own copy of Albia, with its own creatures and history. When Zeetures starts, it asks which to open.

- **New World** (File menu, or the first window): name it, and choose what to start from. Eden is a fresh Albia; other worlds you have installed are listed too.
- **Open World** (File menu): switches worlds. The one you leave is saved first.
- **Always open this world at launch:** a tick box in the first window, and File > Open This World at Launch in the game. Zeetures then starts in that world without asking. Untick the menu item to be asked again. If Zeetures closed while opening the world last time, it asks, so that you are never stuck.

### What is saved

- Every creature: its body and chemistry, what it has learned, its words and names, its memories of places and others, its temperament, and its life story.
- Eggs, photos, gravestones, and family records.
- The objects in the world, where they are and as they are: food, toys, plants, lifts, boats, and those you added.
- The date, the germs, your favourite places, and which creatures may breed.

### When it is saved

- When you quit, and when you switch worlds.
- Every five minutes, unless you change it (File > Autosave; from every minute to only when quitting).
- Whenever you choose File > Save World. Save World As makes a copy under a new name, photos and all, and you go on in the copy.

Worlds saved by earlier versions open in later ones. If a saved game cannot be read, or some creatures in it cannot be loaded (a breed's files removed, perhaps), Zeetures keeps the saved game safe beside the world, says so, and lets you quit and put things right.

## Updating Zeetures

Zeetures can update itself, if it was installed with Zeetures-Setup.exe on Windows, from the disk image on a Mac, or as the AppImage on Linux. Your worlds are never touched.

- **Checking:** Zeetures looks for a new version when it starts and every six hours while it runs. Help > Check for Updates looks now, and so does the button in About Zeetures. Help > Check for Updates Automatically turns the regular checks off and on.
- **When one is out,** a window says so. Update Now downloads it while you play; then Restart Now saves your world, installs it, and starts again in the same world, or When I Quit installs it as you leave. What's New lists the changes. Not Now leaves it until next time.
- **Installing automatically:** with Help > Install Updates Automatically ticked (it starts unticked), new versions are downloaded without asking and installed when you quit.
- **Safety:** each download is checked against the release's own record of it, and thrown away if it does not match.
- **Other copies:** the .zip and .tar.gz, or a copy built from the source, cannot update themselves. They are told about a new version, with a button to the download page.

The check asks GitHub, where Zeetures is published, and sends nothing about you or your worlds.

## When something is wrong

- **Nothing happens when I type.** Click on the world, so that no box in a window has the keyboard.
- **Creatures take no notice of me.** They may be asleep, unconscious, or too far away; the hand may be invisible (Pointer toolbar); or they may know none of your words yet. See [[Talking to creatures]].
- **No eggs hatch.** The world may be at its limit, set at the foot of the [[Hatchery]]. Eggs in water wait until they are on dry land.
- **Nobody breeds.** See the list in [[Breeding and eggs]]: their age, their feelings for each other, kinship, the Hatchery's fertility setting, the season, and radiation all count.
- **A creature is unconscious.** It has run out of energy. Food dropped on it is eaten for it. See [[Illness and medicine]].
- **The game runs slowly.** Large populations are the main cause: lower the limit in the [[Hatchery]]. At more than 1x, the Speed box's tooltip says when the world cannot keep up.
- **An add-on does not look right.** Its pictures may be missing: import the whole package, not only its COB. See [[Breeds, objects, and genomes]].
- **The wrong game folder.** File > Creatures 2 Folder chooses another, used from the next start.
- **Reporting a bug.** Help > Zeetures on GitHub opens the project's page. Say which version you have (Help > About Zeetures) and what you did. On Windows, the file zeetures-log.txt in Documents\My Games holds the program's messages from its last run.

## What differs from Creatures 2

If you know the original game, these are the main differences.

- **Minds.** Creatures run Zeetures's own mind, not the original brain. Their genomes' chemistry, organs, and instincts run as written.
- **Words.** Creatures are born knowing none, and learn each by hearing it. You can give your own names to things.
- **Temperament, relationships, and kinship** are new.
- **Every kit is on from the start**, with no power-ups to find, and all are inside the game's window.
- **Population.** There is no limit of 16: you set the limit yourself (150 to begin with).
- **The world is alive.** Temperature, air pressure, wind, radiation, scents, germs, and the soil's nutrients spread from room to room, and outdoor light follows the day.
- **Germs.** The diseases the original manual described, and the game never had, are here.
- **Eggs hatch wherever they lie**, not only in the incubator, as long as they are on dry land.
- **New ways through the world**, and ways out of the sea. See [[Getting about, and new places]].
- **Not here:** exporting a creature, for now.

- **Invisible:** creatures cannot see the hand.

The Hatchery, the Agent Injector, and the kits. See [[About the kits]].

## The status bar and notices

From left to right, along the foot of the window:

- **Pictures of the season, the time of day, and the temperature,** each named when the pointer rests on it. Click the temperature to change between Celsius and Fahrenheit.
- **The day and year.** See [[Time, seasons, and weather]].
- **Minimised kits,** as small buttons that bring them back.
- **Notices,** newest on the right.
- **The selected creature's health:** a bar from skull to smile, for its weakest organ; it flashes while the creature is unconscious or an organ is failing. Beside it, its heart beats, faster with fright or effort.

Births, deaths, pregnancies, eggs laid, illnesses and recoveries, faints, and changes of shape appear here as they happen.

- **Click** one to go there: to the egg, the creature, or its gravestone.
- **Right-click** one to dismiss it, to clear them all, or to open the life log.
- **+N** lists the older ones that do not fit.
- **To hide a kind of notice,** untick it at the foot of its kit (births in the Breeder's Kit, deaths in the Graveyard Kit, illness in the Health Kit, changes in the Owner's Kit). The life log still records it.

The whole record is in the [[Chronicles Kit]].

## Creature's View

View > Creature's View, or the toolbar's Eye View, shows the world as the selected creature sees it. The view centres on whatever it is paying attention to, and a line beneath says what it is doing.

The eye is its own: a norn's round pupil, a grendel's slit, an ettin's coarse sight, in the creature's colouring.

- The pupil narrows in bright rooms and widens in the dark, and with fear.
- The lids blink, droop when it is sleepy, and close when it sleeps.
- Its sight reddens and doubles when it is hurt or ill, and ripples under water.

# Living with creatures

## The hand

The hand is you, as far as creatures know. They can see it, hear it, be touched by it, and learn what to expect of it.

### Using things

- **Left click** a button, lift, machine, or toy to use it.
- **Right click** to pick a thing up, and again to put it down. Throw it by moving as you let go. You can take things that creatures are carrying.
- **You may not drop a thing** inside rock, or in a few places that would trap it: the hand shows that it cannot, and plays a sound.

### Teaching by touch

With Teach on (Pointer toolbar), a small angel or devil beside the hand shows what a click on a creature will do.

- **Tickle:** click its head. It is a reward, and a comfort.
- **Slap:** click lower down. It hurts, and a creature slapped often may learn to keep away from the hand.

What creatures learn from touch is the same as what they learn from anything: whatever they were doing just then is followed by something good or bad. So reward at once. Touch is most powerful with the young, whose temperaments are still being shaped (see [[Temperament]]).

### Moving creatures

- **Push** (Pointer toolbar): an arrow shows which way a click on a creature's body or legs will shove it: a short hop, away from the hand.
- **Pick up:** right-click a creature to lift it, and right-click again to set it down.

### Feeding by hand

Drop food on an unconscious creature and it is eaten for it, which can bring a starving creature round.

### Not being seen

Invisible (Pointer toolbar) hides the hand from creatures, so that you can watch without drawing a crowd. The hand is drawn see-through while it is hidden.

## Talking to creatures

### How to speak

Type while a creature is near and press Return. Every creature that is awake within earshot hears: about 350 pixels to either side and 150 above or below, at actual size. Ctrl+S says the same thing again, and Speech History (Favourites toolbar) can repeat anything said lately.

### What they do

- **They look.** Spoken to or touched, a creature stops what it is doing, turns, and looks at the hand for a few seconds.
- **They answer.** The nearest two, or whomever you named, say your last word back: in their own tongue until they have learned it, and as the word itself once they have. Babies always answer in their own tongue. Where you said nothing they could know, they babble.
- **They understand names.** Say a creature's name, and only that creature is meant. Every creature has a name; change it in the [[Owner's Kit]].
- **They look at what you name.** Say the name of a kind of thing they know the word for ("food", "toy", "lift"), and they look at the nearest.

### Their own tongue

Creatures are born knowing no words. Until they learn yours they speak Nornish, or the grendel or ettin dialect, in sounds that always mean the same. Learned words and their own tongue mix in one sentence, so you can hear a creature's language grow.

### How words are learned

Each hearing makes a word more familiar, until it is known. Children learn fastest, then adolescents; the senile slowest.

- **Teaching machines and laptops:** one press teaches a word to a child, adolescent, or youth looking at the screen or standing close; babies and adults need another. Others in earshot learn it more slowly.
- **The hand:** about three times for a child or an adult, four for a baby or the old, and seven for the senile.
- **Other creatures:** each time they use a word they know. Family are heard twice as well, so words pass down a line.
- **Things in use:** creatures name what they have just used, most of all when a baby or child of their family is watching.
- **The Stone of Ancient Knowledge:** a norn that kisses it knows every word at once.

The [[Mind Kit]]'s Words page lists what the selected creature knows, and what it is part of the way to knowing.

### Telling them what to do

The verbs are **stay, push, pull, stop, come, run, get, drop, rest, left, right, eat,** and **hit**. Use one alone, or with the name of a thing: "push toy", "get food", "come". On its own, "come" means to the hand.

A verb is a suggestion, not an order. Whether a creature does it depends on:

- **Knowing the word,** and the instinct in its genes to follow it. Babies have the instinct for push, pull, come, get, rest, and eat; more follow as it grows. Grendels and ettins follow only the verbs their genomes have instincts for.
- **What it needs.** A starving creature eats first.
- **Its temper.** The good-tempered and sociable listen more.
- **Being named.** A creature listens a little better when you use its name.
- **How you have answered before.** Tickle it within a few seconds of doing as it was told, and it will again. Unpraised, it heeds you less and less. Slap it for doing as it was told, and it will be slower to again; slap it for not listening, and it listens better next time.

## Names of your own

You can teach creatures words that are not in the game at all: "mummy" for your hand, "zorb" for a ball.

### How to teach one

- **A name for your hand:** tickle the creature, then type the name within ten seconds.
- **A name for a thing:** rest the pointer on it, or hold it in the hand, and type the name.
- **Say it a few times.** Ctrl+S repeats it. Most have it by the third time, babies and the old by the fourth, and the senile by the seventh.

Say one new word at a time. The word may be in a sentence with words the game knows, and everyday words (hello, good, please, the, and so on) are not taken for names; but if there are two strange words, neither is learned.

If you are showing nothing and have not touched it lately, a creature takes the word for whatever it is looking at beside the hand, or else for the hand itself.

### What they do with it

- They use your name for the thing from then on, in place of the game's word.
- They look at the thing when you say it, and it works with verbs: "push zorb", "come mummy".
- They teach each other: whoever hears the name can learn it, and family learn it twice as fast.

### Good to know

- A name stands for a kind of thing (every ball, not that ball). To name one creature, use the [[Owner's Kit]].
- A word comes to mean one thing, and a thing to have one name. Teach a different name, and the old one fades.
- A creature keeps 40 names of its own at most, and drops the least familiar.
- The [[Mind Kit]]'s Words page lists them, with those half learned.

## Thoughts, faces, and signs

### Thought bubbles

When a creature sets about something new, a bubble shows why, in pictures: the need on its mind, the thing or creature it is thinking of, or which way it means to go. View > Thought Bubbles chooses every creature, the selected creature only, or none.

- **Needs:** an apple for hunger, a sun for heat, a snowflake for cold, Zs for sleep, a battery running low for tiredness, people for company, a frightened face for fear, a jagged burst for pain, a sticking plaster for injury, and rising bubbles for air.
- **An apple and a thing:** it remembers that food, and is going to it. **A nose:** it is following the smell of food.
- **A thing and a question mark:** it wants one, and is looking for it.
- **A heart and a face:** it is going to find its mate. **Fear and a face:** it is keeping away from that creature.
- **A need and a house:** it is going to a favourite place. **A need and a cross:** it is leaving somewhere it dislikes.
- **Feet and a question mark:** it is exploring. **An arrow:** which way.
- **An exclamation mark and a thing:** a surprise. That did far more than it expected.

### Speech bubbles

Words in a bubble are spoken. View > Speech Bubbles hides them; the voices go on (Bibble, under File > Sound, turns those off).

### Other signs

- **Zs drifting from the head:** asleep.
- **Stars round the head:** unconscious.
- **Faces:** creatures show anger, distress, contentment, and the rest as they feel them. A few add-on breeds, and those made for the first game, have fewer faces.
- **Sneezing and coughing:** it is ill. See [[Illness and medicine]].

## What creatures need

Every creature has seventeen needs, made by its own chemistry: pain, pleasure (the want of it), hunger, cold, heat, tiredness, sleepiness, loneliness, crowding, fear, boredom, anger, sex drive, injury, want of air, thirst, and stress.

- Each need pushes harder the larger it grows, and the severe ones push hardest of all: a creature that is both bored and starving thinks of food.
- How much each matters differs from one creature to the next. A hot-tempered one feels anger more; a sociable one, loneliness.
- Contentment is how far all its needs, together, are from their worst.

See a creature's needs as its body makes them in the [[Health Kit]] (Drives), and as its mind feels them in the [[Mind Kit]] (Feelings). The season and its life stage change some of them.

## How creatures learn and choose

### Choosing

Several times a minute, or when something interrupts it, a creature weighs everything it could do: use something it can see, go to somewhere it remembers, follow a scent, rest, explore. For each it expects some change in its needs, from its own experience, and it takes account of:

- how far away it is, and how patient the creature is about walking;
- curiosity: things that are new, or that it is still learning about;
- how used it has become to something that never does anything;
- what it was already doing, as it does not drop one thing for another lightly;
- you, if you have just spoken to it.

Then it picks, usually the best. The [[Mind Kit]]'s Choosing page shows all of this.

### Learning

- **By doing.** Afterwards it compares what changed with what it expected, and corrects itself. Every creature's opinions are its own.
- **By watching.** Creatures learn from what they see others do, what it does to them, where they eat, and where they are hurt or fall. Family teach the most, and the young learn the most.
- **By instinct.** Genes give weak first guesses, which experience overrules.
- **By fruit.** Creatures can learn that the volcano's fruit eases pain, and that rotten fruit hurts.

### Remembering places

Creatures build a map: rooms they have been in, where food was found, which lift or boat goes where, where they felt better or worse than usual. They return to places that eased a need, and keep away from places where they were hurt, or could not breathe. A place long unvisited becomes interesting again. See the Mind Kit's Memory map and Places.

### What is inherited

Nothing learned is passed on, but readiness to learn is. See [[Temperament]].

## Temperament

Every creature is born with some of each of seven traits. Two creatures from the very same genome still differ.

- **Boldness:** from shy to bold. The bold feel less fear, and are drawn to the new and to strangers.
- **Curiosity:** new things are worth more, and boredom bites harder.
- **Energy:** from placid to energetic. The energetic walk faster, and grow restless sooner.
- **Sociability:** from aloof to sociable. Loneliness bites harder, crowds matter less, and bonds grow faster.
- **Temper:** from easygoing to hot-tempered. Anger bites harder, harm sours bonds more, and fights are forgiven more slowly.
- **Reactivity:** from steady-nerved to reactive. Moods swing, stress bites harder, and bad outcomes are learned faster than good ones.
- **Steadiness:** from flighty to steady. The steady stick with what they are doing.

### Where it comes from

- **Parents:** less than half of it is passed on; the rest is chance.
- **Its genes' chemistry:** a genome that raises fear or anger strongly makes a timid or quick-tempered creature. Bred, mutated, and custom genomes can differ a good deal from the usual.
- **Its childhood:** hurts and frights, comfort and play, fights, and how trying new things turned out. Babies are the most changeable, then children and adolescents; from youth it is settled.

### The mind's own genes

Eight more settings are inherited in the same way: how fast it learns, how decisive and how patient it is, how much it wanders, and how readily it learns about danger, food, water, and company. A line that is quick to learn what keeps it alive tends to last, so over many generations a population can change.

There is also a seasonal rhythm: how strongly a creature fattens in autumn and saves itself in winter.

All of this is on the [[Owner's Kit]]'s Temperament page.

## Relationships

Each creature remembers every other it has met, and feels something about each. Feelings go one way: A may adore B, who cannot stand A.

- **Help** (a kiss, a tickle, food or a toy brought) builds liking.
- **Harm** takes it away three times as fast, and leaves fear.
- **Time together** makes others familiar, and liked, if the creature was lonely.
- **Time apart** lets feelings fade, slowest of all between mates.

### What feelings change

- Whom a creature goes to, avoids, defends, or shares food with.
- Friends nearby ease fear and stress; a mate who is away is missed.
- Two who dislike or fear one another do not conceive; a liked partner makes it likelier.
- Those raised together as children are less drawn to each other later.

### Kindness

Grown creatures look after babies, children, the senile, the ill, and the poisoned of their own kind: they come when they smell that care is needed, tickle them, and bring food or a toy. Friends share food with a hungry friend, and defend one who is hurt.

The [[Owner's Kit]]'s Relationships page shows it all as a map and a list.

## Growing up and growing old

There are seven stages: baby, child, adolescent, youth, adult, old, and senile. How long each lasts is in the genome; a usual norn lives about nine or ten hours of play. Each creature ages at a slightly different pace.

- **Babies** speak only their own tongue, are tended by others, and take things in by watching.
- **Children** learn words fastest, explore the most, and say one word at a time.
- **Adolescents** can breed, though not often. Temperament is settling.
- **Youths and adults** are at their strongest. Adults are the most fertile.
- **The old** are less fertile, learn more slowly, and need more rest and company in winter.
- **The senile** cannot breed, learn slowly, and are cared for by others.

To make a creature older or younger, see the [[Owner's Kit]], Registration.

### Death

Creatures die of old age, starvation, illness, poison, drowning, or an organ failing. The life log and the gravestone give the cause. A dead body lies for a while and then floats away. Its genes are kept, so it can still be a parent: see the [[Breeder's Kit]]'s Insemination page. See also the [[Graveyard Kit]].

## Food and hunger

Food is chemistry. Starch, fat, and protein become glucose, the glucose becomes energy or is stored, and the stores run down. Hunger rises with an empty store, not an empty stomach, so some foods feed far better than others.

- **Fruit bushes** grow all over Albia and follow the season. Their fruit buds, ripens, goes overripe, falls, and rots.
- **Rotten fruit** hurts a little, feeds the germs, and can make a creature ill; creatures learn to leave it. Rotting also feeds the soil, and bushes grow faster where the soil is rich, except those in pots.
- **Volcanic fruit,** from the ember-red bushes by the volcano, is food and also a cure. See [[Radiation and heavy metals]].
- **Other food:** everything the game itself calls food, and the gel drops from the lab's dispenser, a treat for anyone who gets there.
- **Dispensers:** say "push" to a creature beside one, and tickle it when it does.

Creatures remember where they ate, follow the smell of food through doorways, eat ahead of a long time without, and find food less pleasant the fuller they are. A starving creature with no food about leaves for somewhere else, by lift or boat if it must.

The [[Ecology Kit]]'s Census shows where food is short.

## Sleep and rest

Sleepiness grows in the dark and with time, and only sleep cures it. Tiredness comes from effort, and rest cures it. A creature will not stay asleep while it is too hungry, or in water.

Most sleep at night, when outdoor light goes. The sleeping hear nothing, so speak to them once they are awake.

## Breeding and eggs

### Who can breed

Two creatures of one kind and opposite sexes may mate from adolescence on. They find each other partly by scent. A kiss may lead to a pregnancy, or may not: the chance depends on

- **her fertility just then,** which rises and falls in a cycle, and how receptive she is;
- **both ages:** adults most, youths less, adolescents and the old little, babies, children, and the senile not at all;
- **their feelings:** none at all if either dislikes or fears the other, and more the better liked;
- **kinship:** parents and children, brothers and sisters, and other close kin do not breed while either has an unrelated partner alive;
- **the season:** more in spring and less in winter;
- **radiation:** the more either has lately been in, the less;
- **you:** the Hatchery's "All creatures fertile", the Infertile box in the Breeder's Kit, and the limit on the number of creatures.

### The child

The child's genome is made from both parents', switching from one to the other along its length, with a few small mutations. Its temperament, mind genes, and seasonal rhythm take after both too. She carries it, then lays an egg.

### Eggs

- An egg hatches where it lies after a while, or at once in the incubator. In water it waits until it is on dry land.
- A notice on the status bar follows it: click to see it.
- "Find eggs in world" in the [[Hatchery]] goes from one to the next.
- Radiation around an egg, or around its mother, adds mutations, which the child can pass on.
- At the limit on creatures, eggs wait.

### Helping

The [[Breeder's Kit]] shows readiness, has aphrodisiacs, can inseminate a female with any creature's genes, and can lay an egg of any breed you have.

## Illness and medicine

### Germs

Germs live in dark caves, in rotting food, and on grendels, who carry them without falling ill. They seep from room to room, and light kills them. Norns and ettins catch them from a room, from rotten fruit, or from a sneeze.

There are four illnesses to begin with: a sneezing cold and a sleeping sickness from caves, a cough from rotting food, and a fever from grendels. Now and then a changed strain appears, against which old immunity does not work.

An illness is a nuisance more than a danger to a creature that is well fed. It lasts several minutes of play while the body makes antibodies, and then the creature is immune for a time.

### What helps

- **Warmth, food, and vitamin C** speed recovery.
- **Others' care:** the ill give off a scent, and their own kind come to comfort and feed them.
- **The Doctor page** of the [[Health Kit]] has medicines, each described there.
- **The light:** carry the ill out of the cave they caught it in.

### Unconscious

A creature out of energy falls down with stars round its head. Give it food (drop it on the creature), or an injection from the [[Science Kit]]'s Syringe, and mend the cause: out of the water, warm, fed. If nothing is done, its organs wear and it may die. Even then, for half a minute, the [[Science Kit]] may bring it back.

### Watching it spread

The [[Ecology Kit]]'s map shows every germ in every room, with the ill ringed in red.

## Radiation and heavy metals

Radiation comes from the volcano's chamber and from high in the sky, and spreads a little way through doorways.

- **Heavy metals:** strong radiation, and some poisoned foods, leave heavy metals in the body. They do not go away by themselves. They are felt as pain, the more the worse, and wear an organ; after days at a high level a creature can die.
- **The cure is EDTA,** which only the volcano's fruit carries. One ripe fruit clears about a seventh of the most a creature can carry, and soothes the pain at once. The [[Health Kit]]'s Doctor has the fruit too.
- **Creatures can learn the cure:** by eating it when in pain, or by watching another. Those who know it seek it, and bring it to others in pain.
- **Fertility:** the more radiation a creature has been in lately, the less likely it is to conceive. Half of the effect goes in a game day.
- **Before birth:** radiation around an expectant mother, or around an egg, adds mutations to the child.

## Norns, grendels, and ettins

- **Norns** are the sociable ones, and the ones most breeds are of.
- **Grendels** hatch from a pile of eggs in a chamber of their own, where the next begins to hatch when none is left alive. They carry germs without falling ill. They have the same kind of mind as norns, with their own genomes: they learn, talk in a dialect of their own, and care for their young.
- **Ettins** have a mind of the same kind too, and a dialect of their own. When none is alive, the egg machine by the volcano lays one of their eggs.

Each kind breeds with its own. The world's own grendel eggs hatch either sex, and either standard or Bony grendels (Boney Grendels by Creature Labs); ettin eggs hatch either sex too. In the [[Hatchery]] all three have their shelves.

# The world

## Time, seasons, and weather

- **A day** is half an hour of play at normal speed, in five parts: dawn, morning, midday, dusk, and night.
- **A season** is four days: two hours of play.
- **A year** is four seasons, spring to winter: eight hours.

A new world begins in spring. Outdoor light follows the day, and lamp-lit rooms, caves, and buildings keep their own.

### What the seasons do

- **Spring** is the breeding season, and bushes blossom.
- **Summer** is for play: creatures explore more, the young most of all.
- **Autumn** brings the best crops. Creatures feel hungrier than they are, and fatten.
- **Winter** makes them sleepier and lonelier. They breed less, save their strength, and keep warm in company.

Each effect is stronger or weaker with the creature's own inherited rhythm and its age: breeding matters to the grown, company to the young and old, rest to the old, and play to the young.

### Weather

Water rises, clouds form and drift with the wind, rain falls, and storms come and go with lightning. Storms raise the air pressure, and wind blows from high pressure toward low. Bees and butterflies feel it too.

## Light, warmth, air, and soil

Albia is a map of rooms, joined by doorways, and each has its own conditions. They matter:

- **Light:** the dark makes creatures sleepy, and germs live in it.
- **Temperature:** spreads from fires, the volcano, and heaters. Creatures feel cold and heat, and huddle for warmth in winter.
- **Air pressure and wind:** see [[Time, seasons, and weather]].
- **Radiation:** see [[Radiation and heavy metals]].
- **Organic nutrients:** rotting food and the dead feed the soil. They break down into minerals, faster where it is warm, and seep downhill.
- **Inorganic nutrients (minerals):** fruit bushes (not those in pots) grow faster where there are more, and draw them up.

See any of them on the [[Ecology Kit]]'s map, and watch up to seven rooms on its Monitor.

## Scents

Rooms hold scents, which drift through doorways and fade. Creatures smell the room they are in and the ones next to it.

- **Food:** the hungry follow it to food out of sight.
- **Mates:** grown creatures give off the scent of their kind and sex. It stirs the other sex, who follow it when the mood is on them.
- **Care:** babies, children, the senile, the ill, and the poisoned give it off, and grown creatures of their kind come.
- **A creature's own:** each has a scent of its own, by which others can find it again.

Show any of them on the Ecology Kit's map; "Tint the world too" colours the main view.

### Making your own

The rules are in the plain-text file pheromones.txt. Its own notes explain every line, and it has examples to switch on: alarm, danger, kindness, and trails. Take a copy from Zeetures's page on GitHub (in its additions folder), change it, and put it in your Zeetures folder (see [[Where things are kept]]). It is read each time a world opens.

## Getting about, and new places

### Lifts, boats, and the rest

Creatures learn each lift, boat, and teleporter by riding it, and remember where it goes. Click a call button to send for one, and click the lift or boat itself to set it off.

- **The submarine** can be called to any of its three docks with the button there, and takes swimmers aboard as it passes.
- **The flying fox,** a basket on a rope, runs between the deck west of the stone arch and the desert lift's scaffold.
- **The glass tube** from the domed lab to the gene lab is walked.
- **The lab lift,** a glass pod in the domed lab's shaft, stops at three floors.
- **The treehouse and bamboo lifts** reach farther than they did: the upper hut's deck, the lower walkways, and the ground by the waterfall.

### Ways out of the sea

Swimmers drown if they cannot get out, so there are ways out everywhere. The submarine picks them up; jump pads on the sea bed throw them in an arc onto the nearest shore; the west sea's airlock draws in everyone near it and lets them out in a pool; the east sea's airlock works by itself when someone is in it; and a boat moored at the end of a dock catches those who step off it.

### Worth finding

- **The memorial stone** in the arcade west of the incubator. Only creatures can press it, and it comforts them.
- **The throne statue** in the west cave, which calms everyone about it, and heals those close by.
- **The path behind the waterfall** west of the incubator's pond, and the passage from the volcano to the potion room.
- **The lab's treat dispenser.**
- **The two cactus seed launchers,** hidden at the west end of the glass tube under the Gelsemium pit, for creatures with the determination to carry them to the desert.
- **The gene splicer** in the gene lab. It has power when a world opens; the button in the domed lab turns it off and on (green on, red off). Put a creature in each pod and press the console: the egg is a splice of both, and they do not survive it. With only one pod filled, the egg is a clone.
- **The Metamorph Device** in the splicer room replaces the creature in its cabin with a newly hatched one of the opposite sex, made from its own genome. The lever chooses how the old one ends: at once, or by poison. Only the hand can work it.

### Walls and bridges

New walkways, bridges, and railings join places that were cut off, and close ledges that creatures fell from. Some were inspired by the Plato Project (JayD) and the Walls and Bridges Pack (Helen and NORNGod); none of their files are used.

# The kits

## About the kits

Open kits from the Tools menu or the Applets toolbar.

- **Whom they show:** the kits about one creature show the selected one. Choose another from the list at the top of the kit, with the arrows beside it, or by clicking a creature in the world.
- **Minimise** a kit with the line at the right of its title bar: it becomes a button on the status bar. The cross closes it.
- **Where they were:** kits open next time where you left them.
- **Hover:** nearly everything in a kit explains itself when the pointer rests on it.
- **Notices on the status bar:** tick boxes at the foot of some kits. See [[The status bar and notices]].

### Which kit?

- Start a life: [[Hatchery]], [[Breeder's Kit]].
- One creature's person and family: [[Owner's Kit]].
- Its body and cures: [[Health Kit]]; in full detail, [[Science Kit]].
- Its mind: [[Mind Kit]].
- All creatures at once: [[Observation Kit]].
- The past: [[Chronicles Kit]], [[Graveyard Kit]].
- The place: [[Ecology Kit]]. Things to add to it: [[Agent Injector]].

## Hatchery

Every egg you can hatch, on its shelf: norns, grendels, ettins, and others. Eggs come from the game, from breed packs you have installed, and from genome files you import. Every egg is back on the shelf when you open the Hatchery again.

### Finding an egg

- **The buttons along the top** show all shelves or one, with the number of eggs on each.
- **Search breeds** by name.
- **Sort** by breed slot, by name, by when it was added, by where it came from, or by when you last placed one.
- **Click** an egg to choose it. The card on the right shows its breed, its sex if known, its genome, the ages its pictures cover, and where it came from.

### Hatching it

- **Place in the world** (or double-click): lays the egg by the incubator. It hatches where it lies after a while.
- **Put in the incubator:** hatches it at once.

### Other tools

- **Find eggs in world:** each click goes to the next unhatched creature's egg.
- **Import Genome:** makes an egg from a genome file (.gen), a Creatures 2 egg file (.egg), or an exported creature (.exp), from anywhere on your computer. You can choose several.

### The line at the foot

- **All creatures fertile:** untick it, and nobody conceives or fathers young, except those set fertile one by one in the [[Breeder's Kit]].
- **Limit the number of creatures:** counts norns, grendels, and ettins. At the limit, creatures stop mating, and eggs of every kind wait to hatch until there are fewer. The limit is 150 until you change it, and is the same in every world.

## Owner's Kit

A creature as a person: who it is, whom it comes from, and whom it loves.

### Registration

- **Its name:** type a new one. It is kept with the world, and creatures answer to it.
- **Photo:** its latest, or a button to take its first.
- **Born, died, parents, children, and moniker,** the code that is its own for ever.
- **Life stage:** make it older or younger. It changes a stage at a time, as natural ageing does, and keeps its memories, chemistry, and health.
- **Notes:** write whatever you like about it.
- **Register this birth:** seals its record. The name can still be changed.

### Photos

Take a photo frames the creature where it stands, without the others. Click a small photo to see it, and Delete to remove it.

### Family

A family tree with photos: parents, grandparents, children, and grandchildren, living and dead.

- **Wheel** to zoom, **middle button** to move, and the button to fit the whole tree.
- **Click a photo** to see it large, and page through that creature's photos with the arrows or thumbnails. Back, Esc, or a click outside returns.
- **Go,** on a living creature's card, selects it and goes to it.

### Temperament

- **Seven traits,** each from 0% to 100%. The middle mark, 50%, is average: the share of creatures with less.
- **What life has made of it** since it hatched, and what its genes' chemistry gives it.
- **Its seasonal rhythm.**
- **Its mind genes:** teal for its settings, violet for how readily it learns about danger, food, water, and company.

See [[Temperament]].

### Relationships

- **The map:** the creature in the middle, others nearer the better liked; on the rim, those it has not met. The near half of each line is its feeling, and the far half theirs: blue for liking, orange for dislike, thicker for more familiar. A pink ring is a mate, purple a creature it fears.
- **Wheel or pinch** to zoom, **drag** to move, **Reset view** to start again. Click anyone to select them.
- **The list** has the same in figures: what it feels, what they feel, familiarity, fear, and what they are to each other.

See [[Relationships]].

### Life Story

Its own life in dated lines: hatching, each stage, its first word and how many it came to know, friends made, those it came to dislike or fear, mates, pregnancies, children, photos, faints, and how it ended.

Notices kept here: changes (a creature becoming something else).

## Health Kit

How a creature's body is, and what to do about it.

### Fitness

- **Overall health:** the strength of its weakest organ, as that is what kills. Rest the pointer on it for how many organs it has and their average; beneath it is what is holding it back. The ageing clock wears down in every creature; that one is not an illness.
- **Body temperature, pulse, and life stage.** The first two draw the last two minutes as a line while the page is open. Click the temperature to change between Celsius and Fahrenheit.
- **Illness:** what it has, for how long, and how far its antibodies have come, with what it is immune to for now. For a grendel, what it carries.
- **Weakest organs:** the five worst. The button beneath opens them all in the [[Science Kit]].

### Mind

What it is doing now and its strongest needs, with a button to the [[Mind Kit]].

### Drives

All seventeen needs as its body makes them. The longer and redder the bar, the stronger the need. See [[What creatures need]].

### Doctor

A shelf of medicines. Click one to read what it is for, and Add to the world to put it there; then encourage your creature to take it. Volcanic fruit, the cure for heavy metals, is on the shelf too, and is put beside the creature.

Notices kept here: illness, recovery, fainting, and coming round.

## Graveyard Kit

A gravestone for every creature that has died in this world.

- **Choose a creature** from the list, or step from stone to stone with the arrows, and read when it hatched and died, how old it was, and what it died of.
- **Epitaph:** a few words to remember it by.
- **Photo:** choose which of its photos the grave shows.

Notices kept here: deaths, and those who grew old together.

## Breeder's Kit

### Reproductive

- **Sex drive, fertility, and, for females, receptiveness** as they are now.
- **Infertile:** ticked, this creature cannot conceive or father young. Its own setting always beats the Hatchery's; Follow the Hatchery gives it up again.
- **Pregnancy:** how far along she is.
- **Hormones:** the chemicals behind it all.

### Insemination

For females. She conceives from any creature of her kind, male or female: living, departed, or a genome file. The donor is not touched, as only a copy of its genes is used.

- **Choose the donor:** living creatures, departed creatures, or genetics files.
- **Before you decide:** the two temperaments side by side, and whether they are kin.
- **What she needs:** to be old enough, not pregnant, and not set infertile. Her fertility cycle does not matter.
- **Inseminate:** she is pregnant at once, and lays her egg as usual. Two mothers are as good as a mother and a father.

Genes of creatures that died before Zeetures began to keep them cannot be had.

### Aphrodisiacs

Choose one to read about it and add it to the world.

### Breeds

Every breed you have, by kind.

- **Pick one** to see a whole adult of each sex, the body parts and ages it has, and where it came from. A star marks breeds in your own data folder.
- **Lay an egg of this breed,** of either sex or the one you choose.
- **Rename** a breed, or move it to another slot: its files and genomes are changed to match.
- **Remove** an imported breed (its files only; creatures wearing it use another's parts).
- **Import files** or **a folder:** see [[Breeds, objects, and genomes]].

Notices kept here: births, pregnancies, and eggs laid.

## Science Kit

The body in full detail.

### Physical

Every organ, named from the species' gene notes where they have them, with its health, clock speed, energy, and number of reactions, receptors, and emitters. Rest the pointer on one for its life force and repair.

### dDNA

Every gene in the genome, by organ: reactions, receptors, emitters, half-lives, instincts, pigments, poses, gaits, and the rest. Hover for what a gene does, when it switches on, its generation, and how readily it mutates. Genes for the original brain are marked inert: this creature's mind does not use them, but its young inherit them.

### Biochemistry

A graph of up to six chemicals over the last two minutes. Choose a group, then click a chemical to show it, and click its name under the graph to take it off.

### Syringe

Inject a ready-made mixture, with its purpose and contents listed, or any one chemical in any amount.

### Bringing back the newly dead

For about half a minute after a creature dies, while its body still lies where it fell, the Syringe can bring it back. First the Metabolism Transplant fills its body with fuel; then the Defibrillant starts it again. It comes back out cold, with its failed organs at a quarter of their strength, and comes round once its energy holds: give it another Defibrillant every ten seconds or so until it does. Mend what killed it, or it may die again. Old age cannot be undone.

Take care: the chemistry is real, and a creature can be killed with it.

## Mind Kit

What a creature feels, how it decides, and what it knows. (It shows nothing for creatures running the original brain.)

### Feelings

- **How content it is.**
- **Every need,** as hard as it is pushing the creature now: its level times how much it matters to this creature. The faint bar behind is the body's own level.
- **What is pushing it most,** in words.

### Choosing

- **What it is doing now, and why.**
- **What it weighed** at its last choice, and how much each option should have eased its needs. Rest the pointer on one for where the expectation comes from: which need, how often it has tried, how much was curiosity.
- **How its last choices turned out:** finished, ran out of time, got nowhere, the thing went away, or another need grew too strong.

### Learned

Everything it has tried, kind by kind: what it expects from each action, against its usual result. Green is better and red worse; the larger the dot, the more often it has done it. Names in pink are creatures it knows one by one.

### Memory map

The world as it remembers it: rooms it has been in, brighter for lately; food it remembers; rides it knows; favourite places; places it keeps away from; and where it is now. Hover for details.

### Places

The rooms it remembers, with the time it has spent in each, and how it felt there against how it feels anywhere: what eased, what rose, what it saw, whom it met. It also lists what the creature longs to find again.

### Mood

How content it has been over time, with the strongest need at each moment as a colour beneath.

### Words

- **Words it knows.**
- **Words it is learning,** with how far along.
- **Names of its own,** which you or others taught it. See [[Names of your own]].

## Observation Kit

### Details

Every living creature in a row: name, kind, age, health, pregnancy, what it is doing, and where. Click a heading to sort by it. Click a row to select that creature, and Go to look at it. A long list scrolls.

### Alarms

An alarm watches a chemical in one creature, or in any, and tells you when it goes above or below a level.

- **Add an alarm,** then choose the creature, the chemical, above or below, and the level.
- **When it goes off,** a note appears over the world for ten seconds, with a button to go there. It goes off again only once the chemical has come back.
- Alarms are checked while the kit is closed, and kept for next time. The page also lists those that have gone off.

Good ones to have: "any creature, Hunger, above 200", and "any creature, Pain, above 150".

## Agent Injector

Adds objects (COBs) to the world: toys, plants, food, machines, and anything else players have made.

### Choosing

- **Search,** sort (by name, by kind, recently added, imported first, in the world now, or recently used), and see them as tiles or a list.
- **Details** for the chosen one: what it is, whether you imported it or it came with the game, how many you have added to this world, and what kinds of thing it makes.
- **Double-click,** or **Add to world,** to add one. Objects you add are saved with the world.
- **Remove from world** runs the object's own removal script, so every one of it goes, not only yours.

### Three lists

- **In the injector:** objects to add by hand.
- **Built into every world:** added each time any world opens, and no longer offered. Build into every world moves an object there; Take out of every world brings it back.
- **Uninstalled:** taken out of the injector, with its file kept. Reinstall puts it back.

Some objects of the game's own are built in already, such as the Stone of Ancient Knowledge, the Metamorph Device, cheese, and the toy car and dog.

### Importing

Import takes COB files, zips of them (with any loose pictures and sounds), and old add-on installers. Everything is copied into your own data folder, never the game's. See [[Breeds, objects, and genomes]].

## Chronicles Kit

The history of the whole world.

### History

Every birth, death, pregnancy, egg, illness, recovery, faint, and change, newest first, with its date. Go takes you there, and Grave to the stone. Rest the pointer on a line too long for its column to read it all.

### Statistics

- **Counts:** living, died, and hatched here, for norns, grendels, and ettins.
- **Records:** the longest lives, the oldest living, the most children, the most generations back, the most words known, and the most friends.

## Ecology Kit

The place rather than the creatures.

### Map

The whole of Albia, its rooms coloured by what you choose under Show:

- light, temperature, air pressure, wind, radiation, and organic and inorganic nutrients;
- scents: food, or any scent creatures give off;
- germs, all or by strain, with the ill ringed in red.

Creatures and Food show where each is. Tint the world too colours the main view by the chosen scent.

- **Hover** a room for its figures. **Click** it to go there.
- **Right-click** a room to pin it, up to seven, and again to unpin it.

### Monitor

The pinned rooms over the last quarter of an hour, with what each is now and how it has changed, the part of the day, and when the next begins.

### Census

- Living creatures and eggs, and everything else in the world by kind, with how many can be eaten.
- **Where the food is,** west to east. Red marks a part of the world with two or fewer things to eat.

# Add-ons

## Breeds, objects, and genomes

Zeetures reads the add-ons players have made for Creatures 2 for more than twenty years. All are copied into your own data folder, and the game's own folder is never changed.

### Breeds

[[Breeder's Kit]], Breeds, Import files or Import a folder. It takes zips, old installer programs, COBs that hold breeds, and loose sprites, body data, genomes, eggs, and exported creatures.

- **You are shown what is in it** and which slot each breed will go to before anything is installed.
- **If its slot is taken,** you can put it in the next free one; its files, genomes, and eggs are changed to match. There are thousands of slots.
- **If it is already installed,** it says so.
- **Its eggs** go on the [[Hatchery]]'s shelf, and its objects to the [[Agent Injector]].

Two packs are fetched the first time, from the Eem Foo archive: Boney Grendels (Creature Labs) and Frog Norns V3 (Alex of Norns of the Round Table, from Nornenmeister's V2).

### Objects

[[Agent Injector]], Import. Nearly every old COB runs: of 116 in one large collection, 115 did. If an object's pictures are missing, import the whole package it came in.

### Genomes

[[Hatchery]], Import Genome. Custom genomes' chemistry, organs, and instincts run as written. Lobes they add to the original brain get matching roles in Zeetures's mind (Nova Subterra by Lummox JR; Canny Norns by Lis Morris and Chris Double; and others from their wiring), which the Breeder's Kit lists when you import one.

### Creatures

File > Import Creature brings in a creature exported from Creatures 2 (.exp): its genome, name, sex, and life stage, with its chemistry and mind made anew. The hand holds it: right-click to put it down. Exporting is not here yet.

### What cannot go back

A creature in a slot past the original game's own cannot go back to it.

## Where things are kept

Your worlds, settings, and add-ons are in your Zeetures folder, apart from the game:

- **Windows:** Documents\My Games\Creatures 2
- **Linux:** ~/.openc2e/Creatures 2
- **Mac:** ~/Documents/openc2e Data/Creatures 2

In it:

- **Worlds:** a folder for each world, with its save and photos. Copy a folder to keep a spare. To take a world to another computer, take the Genetics, Images, Body Data, Objects, and Sounds folders too.
- **Genetics, Images, Body Data, Objects, Sounds:** what you have imported, and your creatures' genomes.
- **settings.txt:** what you have set in the menus.
- **kits.ini:** where the kits are, their alarms, and their notices.
- **Additions:** your own versions of Zeetures's world scripts, used in place of the built-in ones of the same name.
- **pheromones.txt:** scents, if you have made your own. See [[Scents]].

Zeetures moves genomes that nothing uses any more to the Trash when a world opens.

One folder up, game-folder.txt holds the place of your Creatures 2, and on Windows zeetures-log.txt the messages of the last run.
